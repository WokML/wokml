M3 — layers, regions, and affine storage

A continuation that outlives its handler, stored in a scheduler cell and resumed later — and how to do it on the RC store without forging an uncollectable cycle. Three questions: how the layers split, what actually lives in a region, and why the storage operation is where "resume at most once" is enforced.

1 · Three layers, and why the cycle decision sits in the middle one

Everything about M3 separates into three layers. The whole point is that the hard decision — how to store a continuation without a cycle — lives in the middle layer, which says nothing about how a continuation is physically encoded. That is what makes it survive the eventual register-based codegen.

The invariant: cycle resolution is defined on the RC ownership graph, never on the continuation's representation. The owned set the Escape classifier computes is the field layout of the register-spill frame — same set, different physical encoding — so any codegen lowering inherits the cycle fix unchanged. A representation-coupled trick ("the interpreter walks the node to find the back-edge") would not survive; a topology rule does.

2 · The cycle, and exactly which edge is uncounted

A self-scheduling fiber holds the run-queue Q (it needs Q to spawn more work), then suspends. Its continuation k captured Q. The scheduler parks k back into Q. Now Q→k and k→Q — a loop. The fix is to make it a loop RC never sees as a counted loop.

fig 2 — the queue is the region; the back-edge into it is the uncounted one

The precise call: the queue's hold on k stays counted (so a parked continuation is not freed underfoot), while k→Q is uncounted because Q is a region — exactly the existing countedRefs filter that already drops isStaticAddr/region addresses. One uncounted edge means no counted cycle, so Bacon-Rajan trial-deletion stays genuinely unneeded — M2's "escape is a lifetime break, not a cycle" reasoning, applied to the queue. And a bonus falls out: while parked, Q's counted hold is k's only counted holder, so rc(k) == 1 — which is the very invariant take needs (§5).

3 · What actually goes in a region? (your batch-dealloc question)

"Store the owned set in a bump region and free it in a batch when out of scope" is right for two of three things and unsound for the third. The split is the whole answer.

region · batch-free

The frame shell + unboxed live state

The continuation node itself (the stacklet slot) and the scalar live values captured inline (U64, tags) are uniform-size and one-shot. Pool/arena them per coroutine; reuse on resume (Perceus/FBIP → zero alloc per step); batch-free the pool when the coroutine is done.

RC cascade · NOT batch

The boxed captured children

The owned set's boxed values are ordinary RC heap objects, possibly shared and aliased. Dropping the continuation runs its pending drops one by one (decrement; free only at rc 0). A wholesale arena wipe would double-free the shared ones. This is the M2b owned-set discipline — it cannot be a memset.

region · batch-drain

The scheduler queue

The run-queue is a region whose lifetime is the scheduler's dynamic extent (§2). At scope exit, drain it: for each parked k run finally then RC-drop it, then free the region's bytes. Batch reclaim of the structure; per-entry cleanup of the contents.

So the honest answer: yes to a bump region — but the region holds the frame shells (and inline unboxed state), and it is the queue. The boxed children of the owned set are not in it; they are RC values reclaimed by cascade, because some of them are alive elsewhere. "Out of scope → batch deallocate" reclaims the shells and the queue bytes in one move; the captured heap values still go through reference counting during the drain.

So drop runs the continuation's pending drop instructions — the set continuationOwned computes from the same Escape classifier that placed the drops — deduplicated by (Unique, Addr) so aliases are freed exactly as their refcount says. That is a refcount-respecting traversal, not an arena reset. The region buys you the shells; correctness on the children is still RC's job.

5 · The affine obligation lives on the storage operation

"Resume at most once across all aliases" is the one guarantee RC will not give you — it permits aliasing. The move that makes it tractable: don't chase the continuation through the heap. Make the two cell operations affine, and let the cell be the choke point every resume must pass through.

fig 5 — the cell holds at most one continuation, at rc 1; take is the single exit

Why this collapses the hard problem. store is a move-in: it consumes the continuation binder, so you cannot resume the value you just stored. take is a move-out: it removes the continuation and asserts rc == 1 (the M2b moveOutCont discipline, lifted from the live Kont to a heap cell). Every resume goes through take, so "≤ 1 resume across all aliases of k" reduces to a local property: "the cell's content is taken at most once." Even if the cell handle is aliased, the content has rc 1 — the first take empties it. The existing Card/Escape machinery proves the single-take statically on the cell, which is far easier than proving affinity over arbitrary heap travel.

Two obligations, kept separate. This — counting resumes — is the affine guarantee. It is distinct from making the resumed tail's effects honest (the load-bearing residual row / "Option B"), which forces runNext's signature to surface the parked effects but counts nothing. M3 needs both, but they are different pillars: the cell discipline here is at-most-once; the row obligation is which effects.
And it is the same rc==1 token throughout. While parked, the queue's counted hold makes rc(k) == 1 (§2). take moves that single token to the caller. The cycle fix and the affine move-out are not two mechanisms — they are one ownership token, observed at park time and at resume time.

6 · Each piece is a primitive compiled code implements

region

arena + tag bit

The queue is a bump arena; the dup/drop pass skips edges into it; drained-and-freed wholesale at scope exit. A compiled scheduler's run-queue is literally this.

owned set

closure-converted fields

The same Escape set becomes the register-spill frame's fields. Drop emits the same pending frees; one-shot → the slot is reused.

affine

move slot + static gate

store/take are moves on a unique-owned slot; the single-take proof is the contify-vs-reify decider — one analysis, shared, not duplicated.

The point: the interpreter (NCont, the region drain, the rc==1 cell) is the oracle for the compiled form. Codegen reuses the owned set, the affine decision, and the region topology — it does not re-derive what the interpreter was secretly doing.
